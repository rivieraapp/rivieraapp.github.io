Riviera — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 12 November 2026

The short version: Riviera has no servers of its own, no analytics, no trackers, and no advertising. Your records stay on your iPhone and leave it only in features you choose to use (iCloud Sync, Apple TV, Shared Garage, exported backups and reports, Calendar and Reminders entries, and buttons or Shortcuts that hand a place, your car's position, your VIN, photos, a file or a message you prepared to another app or website, such as Share, Copy, directions and the recall check) or inside an iCloud or computer backup of your iPhone, if you make one. To work, the app talks directly to Renault, Apple and a few public map, weather and fuel-price services, listed below. The app never sends anything to the developer by itself. Apart from anonymous crash and usage reports that Apple passes on if you opted in to share them, feedback sent through TestFlight, and anything you choose to send (such as an e-mail), the developer receives nothing.

Who we are

Riviera is an independent, community-built iOS companion app for Renault connected vehicles, made by an individual developer. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Renault s.a.s. Contact details are at the end of this page; help with the app is on the support page.

Who is responsible

Riviera is published by Ionuț Andrei Paraschiv, an individual developer in Romania. Contact: riviera.appsupport@icloud.com.

The developer is responsible for the app and for this policy. The developer runs no server, and the app sends the developer nothing by itself. For privacy questions or requests, write to riviera.appsupport@icloud.com. Renault, Apple and the public services listed below handle what they receive under their own policies.

Features that use your iCloud

iCloud Sync and Apple TV are separate switches, off until you turn them on. Shared Garage has no switch: it starts when you tap Share Garage… (or open an invitation). All of them use Apple's iCloud under your own Apple ID; none uses a server run by the developer.

  • iCloud Sync keeps your records, the odometer log, your tyre sets and condition checks (without their photos), your car's name, the places you have named (with their map positions, to about 10 metres) and settings (language, country, currency, units, temperature unit, tank size and alert choices) in step across your own devices, through your private iCloud container. iCloud Sync sends your language only once you have chosen one in Riviera, never your phone's own language. (The Apple TV summary below carries the language the app is shown in, which follows your phone until you choose one.) The scanned files and condition-check photos themselves are not part of it. Records you delete stay in these copies only as the small hidden markers described above, until you use Delete All My Data (which removes them from your own iCloud copies, not from other devices).
  • Riviera on Apple TV. If you switch on Show my car on Apple TV (Settings → Data & Sync), Riviera saves one small summary of your car as a single record in your private iCloud (CloudKit) database so the Riviera app on an Apple TV signed in to the same Apple ID can show it: the car's name as you typed it, its model and year, the address of its picture (left out if it contains your VIN), range, fuel or battery level, charging and lock status (with any open doors), odometer, your lease progress (kilometres left and pace, never money), the next service, up to six of your tracked documents or services (such as insurance or inspection), each with the name you gave it and when it is due, your theme colours, language and units, your car's drive type, and the times the car and the phone last reported. You can separately choose to include the street address where the car is parked and how long it has been there (Include Parking Location); coordinates are never shared. This works whether or not iCloud Sync is on. The summary is stored by Apple in your iCloud and is not sent to, or readable by, the developer; unlike the Shared Garage, it is not kept in iCloud's encrypted fields. The Apple TV app reads only that record and keeps no copy of the summary; it fetches it again every few minutes while it is open, so a summary you remove leaves the TV screen at its next fetch. The car's picture, which the Apple TV app downloads from the address in the summary, may stay in tvOS's own web cache on the Apple TV. A copy that earlier versions left in your iCloud key-value storage is removed. The Apple TV app only displays the summary and cannot control your car. It shows this on the television screen, so anyone who can see the screen can read it, including the parked street address if you turned that on, and the summary is readable by every Apple TV signed in to the same Apple ID.
    Signing out, switching account or entering Demo Mode takes the summary off iCloud but leaves the switches as you set them, so it is published again at the next sign-in. Turning sharing off removes the summary and turns off both Show my car on Apple TV and Include Parking Location on this iPhone; Delete All My Data removes it as well and also resets those two switches and the iCloud Sync switch; another iPhone on the same Apple ID that has sharing on will publish it again until you turn it off there. If your iPhone cannot reach iCloud whenever Riviera tries to take the summary off (signing out, switching account, entering Demo Mode, turning sharing off or Delete All My Data), it retries the next time the app opens or comes to the foreground, or when the iCloud account changes. Deleting the app alone does not remove it.
  • Shared Garage. When you tap Share Garage…, Riviera stores a copy of that car's records (including prices, lease and bank-credit details, subscription entries such as insurance and driving-licence dates, the odometer log and your tyre sets, but never your condition checks or their photos), the car's name (which also titles the invitation), the VIN, the tank size you chose, and the names of places within 100 metres of where this car has parked in its saved parking history (up to 90 days, or 30 if you chose that) or stands now, with their map positions (to about 10 metres), in your private CloudKit database. The records and the car's name are kept in CloudKit's end-to-end encrypted fields, whose keys iCloud manages; Riviera adds no key of its own. Apple can still read the invitation's title (your car's name followed by "Garage"), a label marking it as a Riviera garage, the time of the last change, and the record's name, a code Riviera makes by hashing your Renault account ID and the VIN, which contains neither. Someone in the garage who still uses an earlier version of Riviera can no longer read the garage once a current version has encrypted it, but that version can still write to it: after they add or change records, it saves its own copy of the car's records (with the VIN, and any records that person deleted, with their full contents), that person's own name for the car and a code made from the account and the VIN in the ordinary fields, which Apple can read. That earlier version also puts into that copy every place that person has named, for all their cars, with their positions (to about 10 metres), not only the places near this car's stops; a current version moves those names into the encrypted fields with the rest, and they stay in the garage. A current version merges that copy and moves it into the encrypted fields the next time it updates the garage. This goes on until that person updates Riviera. While the garage stays linked on this iPhone, Riviera updates that copy automatically a couple of seconds after you add or change records, and merges the changes your invitees make into your records on this device. It stays there, even if you cancel or stop sharing, until you use Delete All My Data. The Apple IDs you invite can read and change it; it cannot be made view-only.
    If you accept someone else's invitation, you can merge their records into one of your cars on this device, or discard the invitation, which also takes you out of the share. The shared copy stays in their iCloud, not yours, and whatever you hold for that car (its records and VIN, and the names of places within 100 metres of where it has parked in its saved parking history (up to 90 days, or 30 if you chose that) or stands now) is written back into the shared copy, so the owner and everyone they have invited can see it; with iCloud Sync on, the merged records are also copied to your own iCloud Sync files. Your own name for the car is not written there, and of the place names the others share, your iPhone adds only those near places your car has stopped at. You can leave a garage you joined with Leave Shared Garage: this iPhone stops reading and writing the owner's copy, the records already merged into your car stay on your iPhone, and what you added stays in the owner's copy. Records you delete stay in the shared copy only as the small hidden markers described above.

Diagnostics

Riviera keeps a diagnostics log on your device so that a problem can be understood without asking you to reproduce it. It holds the most recent 160 app events (including lock readings, the times of remote commands, connections to Renault that were refused for their certificate, the outcome of a vehicle-alerts check (its result, the HTTP status, how many alerts came back and the names of the fields Renault sent, never their values), how many drives an import from the Riviera car app brought or skipped, whether it brought a parking reading and, when a code could not be read, checked or saved, the kind of failure, when a saved list of records could not be read in full (which list and how many records, never what they hold), how much of its size limit a Shared Garage takes once it passes 70% (the size only), and when the app launched or moved between the foreground and background) and up to 24 recent visits, with the time of each, to a handful of screens such as Controls, Consumption, Parking History and the maps.

Riviera never uploads this log, and it leaves your iPhone only if you choose to send it, or inside a backup of your iPhone (iCloud or computer) if you make one. Riviera has no server. Three things can send it, and each only acts when you tap it: Export Redacted Diagnostics, Report a Problem and the Send Report button on the "Something went wrong" card. Export lets you choose where the file goes; Report a Problem and Send Report prepare an e-mail addressed to the developer (riviera.appsupport@icloud.com), and nothing is sent until you send it. Export Redacted Diagnostics and Report a Problem show you the complete file first. Send Report builds the e-mail text itself instead of attaching a file: the same diagnostics, plus the time and the text of the error that triggered the card (redacted the same way); if no mail app can open the draft, it copies that text to your clipboard instead. These e-mails are sent from your own mail account, so the developer also sees the address and name you send from and anything you write; the redaction applies only to the diagnostics text.

The file you send holds the recorded events and screen visits plus your iOS version, your iPhone's model identifier (for example iPhone17,2) and screen size, the app version and build, your country, language and units, whether iCloud Sync, Beta and Demo Mode are on, a summary of your car's model, year, powertrain and connected services, the car's last reported lock and door state with its time, the times of unlock reminders and alerts and whether that alert is switched on, the names and end dates of its Renault connected-service contracts, that Riviera sends no lock or unlock command, whether its remote commands work, its last eight remote commands with their times, how many automatic Renault reads the app has made recently, counts of the haptic feedback the app played, skipped because haptics are switched off, or held back, and, in some event lines, the address path of a Renault request (with the VIN and account identifiers masked).

It also holds short summaries of the crash, hang, slow-launch and excessive CPU or disk-write reports that iOS itself gives Riviera on this iPhone (Apple's MetricKit): for each, when it happened, its kind, the app and iOS versions, the exception or signal numbers, or how long or how much (a hang's duration, for example), and up to eight entries of the program's call stack written as code positions (such as "AustralApp +123456"). They contain nothing you typed and nothing that was on screen. Riviera keeps the newest ten summaries with the diagnostics log on this iPhone and never uploads them; they leave the iPhone only inside the file you choose to send, as described above. This is separate from the crash reports Apple may share with the developer if you opted in (see below).

Before an event is saved, Riviera removes anything that looks like an e-mail address, a VIN, a sign-in token, a coordinate pair written with four or more decimals or a number labelled latitude or longitude, and any account or person identifier, whether labelled (such as accountId=) or part of a Renault web address. It also removes the name you gave the car that was selected when the app started, if that name is at least three characters long. A rename, switching to another car or a first sign-in is covered only after the app is restarted. The app never logs your password and is written to avoid logging names, places and street addresses. The removal above covers only the items it lists: it does not recognise other names, place names or street addresses, and because it works by pattern it cannot recognise free text, so read the file before you send it.

You can read the recorded events at any time in Settings → Help & Support → Diagnostics → Diagnostics Log, and the complete file in the review step of Export Redacted Diagnostics. You can clear the log, and the crash and hang summaries with it, with Clear Diagnostics on the Diagnostics Log screen. When you export or report, Riviera also leaves the file it created in the app's cache folder (excluded from backups) until iOS clears it or you run Delete All My Data; Clear Diagnostics does not remove that copy. Delete All My Data removes the log and that file along with everything else.

Where the app sends network requests

Riviera communicates only with:

  • Renault's services — the Gigya sign-in and the Kamereon vehicle API, to operate your car's connected features (vehicle data, and the commands you send), and, only when you tap Check vehicle alerts (Settings → Help & Support → For Testers, shown only after seven taps on the version number in Settings → About Riviera) or Check again after it, one request for the list of alerts Renault keeps for your car, at most once every 10 minutes per car and never in the background; Riviera keeps the answer's result, when it was checked, the HTTP status and the names (never the values) of up to 40 fields Renault's answer carried, and, for each alert, only its identifier, type, severity, title, status, closure reason, mileage and next due date and mileage, never any contact details the answer may hold, until you use Delete All My Data; Renault's public websites for the dealer locator and the accessories catalogue (the dealer locator receives a position rounded to about 100 metres and a search radius, the accessories catalogue receives your car's model codes, the country site you reach shows your market, and neither receives your VIN; the accessories catalogue Riviera has read is kept in memory only, for up to 6 hours and at most four catalogues, one per model and market, so reopening the screen asks nothing again; it is never written to the iPhone, and Delete All My Data empties it); and the web address Renault's API supplies for your car's picture, from which the picture is downloaded (the Apple TV app downloads it too, if you use it). These websites and the picture's host also see your IP address, as with any web request, and the picture is requested from exactly the web address Renault's API supplies. When the iPhone downloads the picture it does not check the address for your VIN or remove anything from it, so if the address contains an identifier such as your VIN, the server that serves the picture receives it. The only check is on the copy of the address saved for Apple TV, which is left out if it contains the VIN. Riviera connects to the Gigya sign-in and the Kamereon vehicle API only when the server's certificate chain is valid and includes one of the certificates or keys built into the app (until June 2027, after which this version falls back to the system's normal checks), and it never follows a redirect from them. If the certificate does not match, for example on a work or public Wi-Fi network that inspects secure connections, nothing is sent: the app says it could not confirm Renault's server identity and suggests updating Riviera or trying another network. Refused connections are noted in the diagnostics log with the server's name and how many times it has been refused since the app started, at the first, second, fourth and eighth refusal and so on, so a connection that keeps being refused does not fill the log;
  • Public data providers — fuel prices from national services in France, Spain, Austria and Italy, from the Competition Council's price monitor in Romania (Monitorul Prețurilor Carburanților) and from Tankerkönig in Germany if you add your own key under Data Sources (the info button on the nearby fuel prices screen); fuel stations, EV chargers and dealers from Open Charge Map and OpenStreetMap (the latter through the public Overpass servers overpass-api.de, overpass.private.coffee and ovp.dresel.systems, tried in turn; the last holds Europe only); and weather from Open-Meteo. Which fuel service is asked depends on where the search is. Riviera works out on the iPhone, from country outlines built into the app, which country the search point is in; no request is made for this. It asks that country's service first, then your Settings country's service if that country's border is within the search area or no more than about 3 km beyond its edge, then OpenStreetMap, which has station locations but no prices. A service that has nothing there or cannot be reached hands over to the next. Fuel, charger and dealer searches carry a position rounded to about 100 metres: your car's parked position, the centre of the map you are viewing or, for the dealer locator and the EV charger search, your phone's position (if you have allowed location). Weather lookups carry only your car's position rounded to about 10 km. Spain's and Italy's fuel data are whole-country files that carry no position; Riviera keeps a copy of each country's station list (public data only) in its cache folder and reuses it for 30 minutes (Spain) or 3 hours (Italy) before downloading it again. Answers from the weather service, the Romanian price monitor, OpenStreetMap and the dealer locator are kept in memory only, for 2 to 30 minutes, and the full-screen map and the EV Charging Stations screen keep, in memory, the fuel stations and chargers they have shown until you close them, so the same search is not sent again. These requests never carry your name, e-mail, VIN or plate, and, as with any web request, the provider can see your IP address. Each request to these providers, and to Renault's dealer locator and accessories catalogue, names the app: it carries the app's name and version, the address of this policy and the developer's support e-mail, so the operator can tell what software is asking and reach the developer. That label says nothing about you, your car or your iPhone beyond "iOS". Your own Tankerkönig key, if you add one under Data Sources, is kept in the Keychain and sent to Tankerkönig whenever Tankerkönig is asked in the order above, which happens only for a search in Germany or, when Germany is your Settings country, near its border; without it, German stations come from OpenStreetMap through Overpass. Riviera includes its own Open Charge Map key, the same for every user, so it does not identify you to Open Charge Map; only if a build has no key of its own does it send instead a key you pasted into an earlier version of Riviera, if there is one (Delete All My Data removes it);
  • Apple services — iCloud (including the optional features above), Apple Maps and the App Store, which the tip sheet asks whether any tip is on sale (in this version none is). Riviera asks Apple's geocoder to turn your car's parked position, and the places in Parking History, into a street address, and uses Apple Maps to draw maps and to search for dealers. Unlike the public providers above, Apple receives exact coordinates for these (the search centre, your car's parked spots and, if you have allowed location, your phone's position once at first sign-in, to find your country). Apple's own privacy policy applies.

While the app is closed, a background refresh (at most about every 15 minutes, when iOS allows it) may contact Renault and update your widgets. The Refresh Car Data action in Shortcuts runs that same refresh, with everything described in this paragraph, when you or an automation you set up runs it, at most once every 10 minutes counted from its last run or the app's last refresh of the car. If you use iCloud Sync, Shared Garage or Apple TV sharing, it may also update those iCloud copies, for example with a refill it detected and saved automatically. While notifications are allowed, it may also contact Open-Meteo with your car's position rounded to about 10 km (when a weather alert is on; the hail alert is on by default) and, when your fuel is low and the low-fuel alert is on (it is by default), the first fuel service in the order above for the place where the car is parked, with that position rounded to about 100 metres, to name the nearest station. Where no price service applies there (a country without one, or Germany without your own Tankerkönig key), the price service of your Settings country, if it has one, comes first when the car is within about 6 km of that country; otherwise that first service is OpenStreetMap: it is still asked, through the Overpass servers named above, although its answer has no prices, so the alert cannot name a station from it. For Spain and Italy nothing is downloaded in the background: the alert uses the copy on the iPhone if it is recent, or goes out without a station. Riviera never tracks your phone's location in the background.

Notifications and Lock Screen cards are created on your device. Riviera does not register for or send push notifications and has no server that could send them; iCloud itself may use Apple's push service to tell your devices that synced data changed.

Some buttons open a website or another app: for example Renault pages, official vehicle portals, dealer websites, Apple Maps, Google Maps or Waze for directions, Apple Weather, the phone dialler or your mail app. Those requests are made by your browser or that app, not by Riviera, and are governed by their own policies. Directions and weather buttons hand the exact position of your car or the place to that app (directions in Apple Maps or Google Maps also carry the name you gave the car; Waze and Apple Weather receive only the position), and the recall check adds your VIN to the link so Renault's page can pre-fill it. To offer your preferred maps app, Riviera checks whether Google Maps or Waze is installed.

Share and Copy buttons (the parked spot, the VIN, an address, a backup, a report, a saved document's attached files, the Breakdown card, a condition check's photos, the trip log, an appointment request, an accessory's catalogue link or part number, and the support address) hand exactly what you chose to the iOS share sheet or clipboard, and where it goes next is up to you. Sharing the parked spot includes its exact coordinates. Prepare a request message on the Service Schedule writes a note for a workshop with your car's model, its odometer reading, the work that is due and up to three days you pick; your VIN is added only if you switch on Include the VIN for that message, which is off each time. You see the whole text first, and Riviera saves nothing of it. It leaves Riviera only when you send it yourself, through the share sheet or by selecting the text and using Copy or Share in the iOS menu. Riviera writes to the clipboard only when you tap a Copy button, or when you tap Send Report and no mail app can open the draft (it then copies the report text, which includes the redacted diagnostics). Copies of the VIN, the parked street address and the report text stay on this iPhone (they are not offered to your other devices) and are cleared from the clipboard after two minutes. It never reads the clipboard. Text you select and copy yourself with the iOS menu, such as the appointment request, is an ordinary iOS copy, which Riviera does not clear.

Riviera's requests to the public providers, to Renault's public websites and to the server of your car's picture write no web cache and no cookies to the iPhone and require TLS 1.2 or later; the Renault sign-in and vehicle-data connections write no cache or cookies either. One exception: if Riviera could not prepare its own copy of your car's picture, the screen in light appearance loads Renault's picture directly, and iOS may keep that answer, and any cookie it sets, in its own cache, under its web address, which contains your VIN if Renault's address does. Riviera keeps its own copy of your car's picture in its cache folder; signing out keeps it. Delete All My Data removes that copy and the national station lists, and empties iOS's cache and cookies for the app, including anything earlier versions left there. Versions up to 1.1 (6) let iOS keep weather, fuel-price, dealer and map answers in that cache, with the car's position to about 100 metres in the request; this version empties that cache and the cookies once, shortly after it first starts.

There are no Riviera servers. No analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking SDKs are embedded, and the app contains zero third-party code dependencies. Separately from Riviera, Apple may give the developer anonymous crash reports and usage figures for the app (for example downloads and sessions) if you have switched on Share With App Developers in iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements, and the feedback, screenshots and crash reports that TestFlight testers choose to send.

Tips to the developer

Tipping the developer is not available in this version of Riviera. The Buy the developer a coffee row at the bottom of Settings opens a tip sheet that asks the App Store which tips are on sale, finds none and says so, so nothing can be bought. Riviera keeps no record of this and sends nothing about it to the developer. Every feature is the same for everyone.

Your controls

  • Delete everything: Settings → Privacy & Security → Delete All My Data removes the data Riviera stores on the iPhone (your records, your car's position history and the names you gave places, your tyre sets and condition checks with their photos, the Breakdown card's plate, your own pictures of your cars, the drives and pairings from the Riviera car app, preferences, cached images and files, and any exported backups, reports or trip logs left in temporary storage), Keychain entries (including the car app's pairing keys), widget data (including the widgets' copy of the car's picture), what Riviera put in Spotlight, pending and delivered notifications and any parking-meter alarm Riviera set, the diagnostics log, your iCloud Sync files and iCloud key-value copy, the Apple TV summary, and the Shared Garage copy you own in your iCloud (CloudKit) together with its invitation, which ends everyone's access to it. It also removes the Calendar events and Reminders whose identifiers Riviera saved, if Riviera has full access to Calendar and Reminders; any it cannot reach stay where they are, and you can delete them in Calendar or Reminders. The "Riviera" calendar and Reminders list themselves stay, since you may have added your own entries; you can delete them there too. The record of which in-app tips you have seen is emptied the next time the app starts.
  • What it does not remove: your Renault account and the data Renault holds; requests already received by Apple and the public map, weather and fuel-price services, which keep their own logs; backups of your iPhone; backup or report files you saved elsewhere, and anything you copied to the clipboard; anything the Riviera car app keeps on your car (Car Link only reads the codes on the car's screen and sends nothing back, so neither Delete All My Data nor Forget this car changes anything there); copies on other devices (a device that still has iCloud Sync on can upload its data again) or held by people you shared with (anyone who already merged a Shared Garage keeps their copy on their device); and a Shared Garage you joined: Delete All My Data neither takes you out of it nor removes what you added, so use Leave Shared Garage first; what you added stays in the owner's copy until the owner deletes it. A few non-personal markers also stay on the iPhone: the Demo Mode setting and the sample car you chose, one-time migration markers, a marker that the app is installed (so the next launch is not taken for a reinstall), a Renault rate-limit timer and a few other technical flags. If an erase could not confirm that a copy in iCloud was removed, a marker saying so also stays, so the app can warn you again.
  • If Riviera cannot confirm that a copy in iCloud was removed, it shows a warning that a copy may remain. It stays on screen for about 12 seconds (longer with VoiceOver) unless you tap it away. When the iPhone is signed out of iCloud, it only warns if its own records show you used iCloud Sync, Apple TV sharing or a linked Shared Garage, so it cannot warn about a Shared Garage you started and then cancelled or stopped sharing, because it keeps no record of it. When you are signed in to iCloud but offline, it always tries to remove the Shared Garage copy and warns if that fails. For the iCloud Sync files and key-value copy it can only check this iPhone: if the iPhone is offline, iCloud finishes the removal when it next connects. To be sure, run Delete All My Data while signed in to iCloud and online. After an erase the app returns to the sign-in screen, and Settings opens again once you sign in or open the sample car.
  • Export your records: Settings → Data & Sync → Export Backup produces a portable file of the selected car's records, as described in the table above. The position history, the command list and your settings (other than the tank size) are not in the Export Backup file, which does include the odometer log and the EV charge log. The history reports leave all of these out, and the EV charge log as well. They do not list each odometer reading, but they print the summary of the odometer log described in the table above and the car's current odometer reading, which is the newest reading in that log when the car does not report one, and for a lease that has ended, the lease-return version uses the log to work out the kilometres driven by the end date. You can see most of them in the app. (The redacted diagnostics file you can export lists your last eight remote commands and a few settings, as described under Diagnostics.) Scans, condition-check photos and other attached documents are included only if you turn on Include documents (off by default).
  • Demo Mode: Try it with a sample car on the sign-in screen, or Settings → Help & Support → Sample Car, shows a fictional car with sample records instead of your own car. What you add or change for the sample car is never saved on the iPhone (a few screens keep it in memory until the app closes). While Demo Mode is on, Riviera puts nothing in Spotlight and takes the Apple TV summary off iCloud, as described above.
  • Lock Screen privacy: Show address on Lock Screen (Settings → Privacy & Security) is off by default. While it is off, the Lock Screen and Dynamic Island card, widgets and StandBy name the parked spot only with a label you gave it, such as "Home"; the Apple Watch and CarPlay card shows no place. (Riviera has no CarPlay or Apple Watch app of its own; iOS can show its widgets and Live Activities there.) The car's name, fuel level, range, lock status, service-due text, consumption, lease progress and the time it has been parked can still be seen while the phone is locked, and a low-fuel notification can name the nearest station and its price per litre. Notifications can show more while the phone is locked, depending on the alerts you have on: your car's name; the name of a tracked document or payment (for example insurance) and when it is due; lease and bank-credit dates and payment days; how much of your mileage allowance you have used and an estimated excess-mileage charge in your currency; which services are due (one notification per car naming up to three, at most once a day); the time your parking meter runs out; that your electric car has reached its charge target, with the charge level; weather at your car, including winter or summer tyre-season advice based on the temperature there (which can name the tyre set your log shows on the car); that the car has been left unlocked for over 15 minutes; and, if you turn them on (both are off by default), that the car has not moved for the number of days you chose (Long-parked reminder, once per stay, worked out from the odometer readings Riviera already takes, without asking Renault for anything) and, 30 days before your lease ends, a reminder to do a condition check, which is not queued if a check before return dated within those 30 days is already saved. Notification reminders (not entries in the Reminders app) are queued with iOS when you save an item. A monthly payment's reminders cover the next 12 months and are topped up daily. A document's reminders are dated, one-time notifications: the first is queued the number of days you chose before the document's expiry date, which can be years away, and if you chose to repeat it, more follow at that interval, at most five in all and none on or after the expiry day. No reminder repeats by itself. Lease and bank-credit reminders, which contain the car's name and contract dates, are queued for the dates leading up to the end of the term, with lease payment reminders for the next three instalments. Queued reminders stay in iOS's notification storage until they fire, you change, renew, mark as paid or delete the item or contract, set its reminder to None, switch off the alert they belong to in Riviera's notification settings (Documents and services, Contract expiry, Lease & credit alerts, Unlocked vehicle, Service due, Parking meter reminder, Long-parked reminder or Condition check before return), or use Delete All My Data. The unlocked-car reminder also goes when Riviera next sees the car locked or moving, the long-parked reminder when Riviera next sees the car has moved, and the condition-check reminder when you save a check before return dated within those 30 days. On iOS 26 and later, Ring as an alarm under Parking meter reminder (off by default) also sets an alarm on this iPhone for the same moment, after iOS asks once for the Alarms permission, so it rings in Silent mode and during a Focus. The alarm says only "Parking meter ends soon", without your car's name or the place, and it goes whenever the parking-meter reminder goes, including when you sign out or use Delete All My Data. Riviera sets no other alarms. Snooze a week on a document or service notification queues one copy of it for a week later (or for the due day, if that comes first). A document's copy goes with its other reminders; a service's copy stays when you change or delete the service item, and goes only when it fires, you switch off Service due or you use Delete All My Data. To hide the text while the phone is locked, set Show Previews to When Unlocked for Riviera in iOS Settings.
  • Siri, Shortcuts and controls: Riviera's Siri phrases and Shortcuts actions that only read data (the car summary, fuel or battery level, range, lock state, service and inspection dates, lease kilometres, tyre pressure) answer from the data already stored on your iPhone, without contacting Renault. Besides the commands described below, one Shortcuts action, Refresh Car Data, contacts Renault: it runs the background refresh described above without opening the app, also while the iPhone is locked, and answers only with a fixed sentence (such as "Car data refreshed."), never a figure, a place or the car's name. Add Fill-up Price adds what you paid to the newest fill-up Riviera logged without a price, after reading the amount, the litres and the price per litre back to you; it needs the iPhone unlocked, does not contact Renault, and does nothing in Demo Mode. The priced fill-up is then saved like any other fill-up, so it goes wherever your fuel records go (iCloud Sync, a Shared Garage), as described above. Open Breakdown Card only opens the app; Scan Receipt opens the app on the receipt scanner. With App Lock on, the Where is my car, service, inspection, lease and tyre answers are withheld. Where is my car needs the phone unlocked; the others can be answered while it is locked if you allow Siri on the Lock Screen in iOS Settings. Actions and Control Center or Lock Screen controls that send a command to your car (flash lights, horn, pre-conditioning) contact Renault and need the phone unlocked; with App Lock on, they also ask for Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode first, and nothing is sent if iOS cannot show that request. On an iPhone with no passcode, where App Lock cannot work, they are sent without that request, as with App Lock off. While App Lock is off, Where is my car also hands the parked position, as exact coordinates, to Shortcuts (even when Siri does not say the address), so a shortcut you build can pass it to other actions or apps. The Open Parking control only opens the app.
  • iCloud Sync, Apple TV, Shared Garage, Calendar and Reminders are opt-in. Switching off Apple TV sharing removes the Apple TV summary from your iCloud. Switching off iCloud Sync stops further uploads but leaves existing iCloud copies in place, and so does ending a Shared Garage you own; those stay until you use Delete All My Data. Calendar and Reminders are used only if you grant access and add an event or reminder yourself; after that, Riviera updates or removes the ones it created when you change or delete the matching service item.
  • App Lock (Face ID / Touch ID / passcode) is optional. With it on, Riviera covers its whole screen, open sheets included, the moment you leave it, so the app switcher does not show your car, and it asks again when the app starts or after you have been away for more than 30 seconds. GDPR
