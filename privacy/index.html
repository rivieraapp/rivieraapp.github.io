Riviera — Privacy Policy

The short version: Riviera has no servers of its own, no analytics, no trackers, and no advertising. Your records stay on your iPhone and leave it only in features you choose to use (iCloud Sync, Apple TV, Shared Garage, exported backups and reports, Calendar and Reminders entries, and buttons or Shortcuts that hand a place, your car's position, your VIN, photos, a file or a message you prepared to another app or website, such as Share, Copy and directions) or inside an iCloud or computer backup of your iPhone, if you make one. To work, the app talks directly to Renault, Apple and a few public map, weather, earthquake and fuel-price services, listed below. The app never sends anything to the developer by itself. Apart from anonymous crash and usage reports that Apple passes on if you opted in to share them, feedback sent through TestFlight, and anything you choose to send (such as an e-mail), the developer receives nothing.

Who we are

Riviera is an independent, community-built iOS companion app for Renault connected vehicles, made by an individual developer. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by Renault s.a.s. Contact details are at the end of this page; help with the app is on the support page.

For privacy questions or requests, write to riviera.appsupport@icloud.com. Renault, Apple and the public services listed below handle what they receive under their own policies.

Features that use your iCloud

iCloud Sync and Apple TV are separate switches, off until you turn them on. Shared Garage has no switch: it starts when you tap Share Garage… (or open an invitation). All of them use Apple's iCloud under your own Apple ID; none uses a server run by the developer.

  • iCloud Sync keeps your records, the odometer log, your tyre sets and condition checks (without their photos), your car's name, the places you have named (with their map positions, to about 10 metres) and settings (language, country, currency, units, temperature unit, tank size and alert choices) in step across your own devices, through your private iCloud container. iCloud Sync sends your language only once you have chosen one in Riviera, never your phone's own language, and a device on which Riviera follows the phone's language keeps following it rather than taking a language chosen on another device. If you go back to iPhone Language, your language is no longer sent; the last one you chose stays in your iCloud until you choose another or use Delete All My Data. (The Apple TV summary below carries the language the app is shown in, which follows your phone until you choose one.) The scanned files and condition-check photos themselves are not part of it. Records you delete stay in these copies only as the small hidden markers described above, until you use Delete All My Data (which removes them from your own iCloud copies, not from other devices).
  • Riviera on Apple TV. If you switch on Show my car on Apple TV (Settings → Data & Sync), Riviera saves one small summary of your car as a single record in your private iCloud (CloudKit) database so the Riviera app on an Apple TV signed in to the same Apple ID can show it: the car's name as you typed it (if you gave it none, its model name, such as "Rafale"), its model and year, the address of its picture (left out if it contains your VIN), range, fuel or battery level, charging status, lock status while the iPhone app shows it (with any open doors), odometer, your lease progress (kilometres left and pace, never money), the next service, up to six of your tracked documents or services (such as insurance or inspection), each with the name you gave it and when it is due, your theme colours, language and units, your car's drive type, and the times the car and the phone last reported. You can separately choose to include the street address where the car is parked and how long it has been there (Include Parking Location); coordinates are never shared. This works whether or not iCloud Sync is on. The summary is stored by Apple in your iCloud and is not sent to, or readable by, the developer; unlike the Shared Garage, it is not kept in iCloud's encrypted fields. The Apple TV app reads only that record and keeps no copy of the summary; it fetches it again every few minutes while it is open, so a summary you remove leaves the TV screen at its next fetch. The car's picture, which the Apple TV app downloads from the address in the summary, may be kept in the Apple TV app's own picture cache on the Apple TV (up to 64 MB, in a folder tvOS may empty when it needs the space). The app empties that cache when it finds nothing shared any more or when the Apple ID changes. A copy that earlier versions left in your iCloud key-value storage is removed. The Apple TV app only displays the summary and cannot control your car. It shows this on the television screen, so anyone who can see the screen can read it, including the parked street address if you turned that on, and the summary is readable by every Apple TV signed in to the same Apple ID.
    Signing out, switching account or entering Demo Mode takes the summary off iCloud but leaves the switches as you set them, so it is published again at the next sign-in. Turning sharing off removes the summary and turns off both Show my car on Apple TV and Include Parking Location on this iPhone; Delete All My Data removes it as well and also resets those two switches and the iCloud Sync switch; another iPhone on the same Apple ID that has sharing on will publish it again until you turn it off there. If your iPhone cannot reach iCloud whenever Riviera tries to take the summary off (signing out, switching account, entering Demo Mode, turning sharing off or Delete All My Data), it retries the next time the app opens or comes to the foreground, or when the iCloud account changes. Deleting the app alone does not remove it.
  • Shared Garage. When you tap Share Garage…, Riviera stores a copy of that car's records (including prices, lease and bank-credit details, subscription entries such as insurance and driving-licence dates, the odometer log and your tyre sets, but never your condition checks or their photos), the car's name (which also titles the invitation), the VIN, the tank size you chose, and the names of places within 100 metres of where this car has parked in its saved parking history (up to 90 days, or 30 if you chose that) or stands now, with their map positions (to about 10 metres), in your private CloudKit database. The records and the car's name are kept in CloudKit's end-to-end encrypted fields, whose keys iCloud manages; Riviera adds no key of its own. Apple can still read the invitation's title (your car's name followed by "Garage"), a label marking it as a Riviera garage, the time of the last change, and the record's name, a code Riviera makes by hashing your Renault account ID and the VIN, which contains neither. Someone in the garage who still uses an earlier version of Riviera can no longer read the garage once a current version has encrypted it, but that version can still write to it: after they add or change records, it saves its own copy of the car's records (with the VIN, and any records that person deleted, with their full contents), that person's own name for the car and a code made from the account and the VIN in the ordinary fields, which Apple can read. That earlier version also puts into that copy every place that person has named, for all their cars, with their positions (to about 10 metres), not only the places near this car's stops; a current version moves those names into the encrypted fields with the rest, and they stay in the garage. A current version merges that copy and moves it into the encrypted fields the next time it updates the garage. This goes on until that person updates Riviera. While the garage stays linked on this iPhone, Riviera updates that copy automatically a couple of seconds after you add or change records, and merges the changes your invitees make into your records on this device. It stays there, even if you cancel or stop sharing, until you use Delete All My Data. The Apple IDs you invite can read and change it; it cannot be made view-only.
    If you accept someone else's invitation, you can merge their records into one of your cars on this device, or discard the invitation, which also takes you out of the share. The shared copy stays in their iCloud, not yours, and whatever you hold for that car (its records and VIN, and the names of places within 100 metres of where it has parked in its saved parking history (up to 90 days, or 30 if you chose that) or stands now) is written back into the shared copy, so the owner and everyone they have invited can see it; with iCloud Sync on, the merged records are also copied to your own iCloud Sync files. Your own name for the car is not written there, and of the place names the others share, your iPhone adds only those near places your car has stopped at. You can leave a garage you joined with Leave Shared Garage: this iPhone stops reading and writing the owner's copy, the records already merged into your car stay on your iPhone, and what you added stays in the owner's copy. Records you delete stay in the shared copy only as the small hidden markers described above.

Diagnostics

Riviera keeps a diagnostics log on your device so that a problem can be understood without asking you to reproduce it. It holds the most recent 160 app events (including lock readings, the times of remote commands, connections to Renault that were refused for their certificate, the outcome of a vehicle-alerts check (its result, the HTTP status, how many alerts came back and the names of the fields Renault sent, never their values), how many drives an import from the Riviera car app brought or skipped, whether it brought a parking reading and, when a code could not be read, checked or saved, the kind of failure, when a saved list of records could not be read in full (which list and how many records, never what they hold), how much of its size limit a Shared Garage takes once it passes 70% (the size only), for each background refresh, nightly check for services that are due, run of the Refresh Car Data shortcut (also when the Refresh Now button starts it) and run of Mark Drive Start or Mark Drive End, whether it read the car, lock and position data and, for a car that can plug in, the battery (yes or no, never the values), how many minutes old Renault's battery reading was, which readings it did not ask for because Riviera had read them less than five minutes before or, for the car's main readings while it is plugged in, less than 30 minutes before, for the two drive actions whether they read the car and, if not, why (in fixed words), whether the reading they got predates the drive's start and whether a drive end is still waiting for the car's position, how many cars the service check covered, which alerts it sent and, if it stopped early, why (runs with the same outcome are folded into one line with a count), the fuel fill-ups detected from the car's fuel level and the fuel rises that were not counted (described below), and when the app launched or moved between the foreground and background) and up to 24 recent visits, with the time of each, to a handful of screens such as Controls, Consumption, Parking History and the maps.

Riviera never uploads this log, and it leaves your iPhone only if you choose to send it, or inside a backup of your iPhone (iCloud or computer) if you make one. Riviera has no server. Three things can send it, and each only acts when you tap it: Export Redacted Diagnostics, Report a Problem and the Send Report button on the "Something went wrong" card. Export lets you choose where the file goes; Report a Problem and Send Report prepare an e-mail addressed to the developer (riviera.appsupport@icloud.com), and nothing is sent until you send it. Export Redacted Diagnostics and Report a Problem show you the complete file first. Send Report builds the e-mail text itself instead of attaching a file: the same diagnostics (without the haptic counts), plus the time and the text of the error that triggered the card (redacted the same way); if no mail app can open the draft, it copies that text to your clipboard instead. These e-mails are sent from your own mail account, so the developer also sees the address and name you send from and anything you write; the redaction applies only to the diagnostics text.

The file you send holds the recorded events and screen visits plus your iOS version, your iPhone's model identifier (for example iPhone17,2) and screen size, the app version and build, your country, language and units, the code of your iPhone's first preferred language (it can include your region, for example en-RO), whether you picked a language in Riviera and whether that choice is dated, whether iCloud Sync, Beta and Demo Mode are on, a summary of your car's model, year, powertrain and connected services (including which of its registration, delivery and manufacturing dates Renault sent, never the dates themselves, and, for a plug-in hybrid, the range, fuel and battery figures the car last reported; for a car that can plug in, also the names of the fields in Renault's last battery answer, its plug and charging codes and how old that reading was), the car's last climate reading (the fields of Renault's answer that carry a temperature, a unit or a time, with their values as Renault sent them, and the inside, outside and target temperatures the app shows from them; a field whose name points to an identifier or the VIN is left out), the car's last reported lock and door state with its time, the times of unlock reminders and alerts and whether that alert is switched on, the names and end dates of its active remote-control contracts with Renault and the total number of contracts, that Riviera sends no lock or unlock command, whether its remote commands work, which of Renault's lock-status and remote-command services the app has set aside as unavailable, and until when, and its last eight remote commands with their times. To explain why the Controls screen shows what it shows, the file also holds the car's model code and the names Renault gives its connection system and head unit, how many cars are on your Renault account and which of them is selected (by its place in the list and its model, never its VIN), whether Renault answered the request for the car's contracts, which version of Renault's service each kind of reading came from, for each remote command whether it is offered and what decides that (for example the Beta Features switch, or a refusal Riviera remembers), whether the car confirmed a pre-conditioning start sent as a trial, and Renault's last answer to each command: which version of the service was asked, the HTTP status, Renault's error code and how the app read it, never the rest of the answer. It also holds how many automatic reads from Renault the app has made in the last hour and the last ten minutes, against its own limits, in total and for each of the three groups the app sorts those reads into; when one of those limits held reads back, a line naming the group and the Renault service in fixed words; whether it has paused them after Renault told it to slow down (and until when), and how often the haptic feedback was played, skipped because haptics are switched off or held back, and how many times the app prepared its haptic feedback. It also lists the last 12 background runs one by one (background refreshes, nightly checks, runs of Refresh Car Data and runs of Mark Drive Start or Mark Drive End that read the car), each with its date and time, its kind, whether Low Power Mode was on, whether Background App Refresh was allowed, turned off or restricted for Riviera, and whether the widgets were asked to update, and it says how many automatic reads of each kind Riviera held back in the last 24 hours because the same reading had been fetched less than five minutes before. Some event lines also carry the address path of a Renault request (with the VIN and account identifiers masked).

It also holds short summaries of the crash, hang, slow-launch and excessive CPU or disk-write reports that iOS itself gives Riviera on this iPhone (Apple's MetricKit): for each, when it happened, its kind, the app and iOS versions, the exception or signal numbers, or how long or how much (a hang's duration, for example), and up to eight entries of the program's call stack written as code positions (such as "AustralApp +0x1e240"). They contain nothing you typed and nothing that was on screen. Riviera keeps the newest ten summaries with the diagnostics log on this iPhone and never uploads them; they leave the iPhone only inside the file you choose to send, as described above. The same list holds iOS's own daily figures for how long the app's launches and hangs took, as time ranges with a count for each (the newest three are kept); they are kept and sent in the same way. This is separate from the crash reports Apple may share with the developer if you opted in (see below).

The file's headings and fixed wording are in English, whatever language the app is set to; a few lines (for example the title of a remote command that failed, or an error text from iOS, CloudKit or Renault) can be in another language. A section called Consumption lets the developer see why the Consumption chart looks as it does, or why it is empty, and what the Drives section has to work with. It holds counts, yes/no flags, days and the tank size. The counts are how many fill-ups are saved (and how many of those are full, partial, detected automatically, without an odometer reading, or left out as possible duplicates) and how many were deleted; how many stretches between two full tanks the chart can use, how many were set aside as implausible and how many fill-ups without an odometer reading fall outside every stretch; how many drive estimates from earlier versions of the app it keeps and how many of them it shows; and, for the Drives section, how many readings it keeps for the car, how many drives it worked out from them and how many stretches it set aside, by reason, and which state the section is in. The flags say whether the car's last report carried a fuel level, a range and an odometer reading (in this section only yes or no, never the readings; the section also says how many hours ago the report was made), whether the app has an earlier fuel level to compare the next one with (and whether an odometer reading was saved beside it), and whether a one-time clean-up of older consumption data has run. The tank size is given in litres, with where that figure comes from (your own setting, the model's factory figure, or the largest fuel level the car has reported). The days are dates without a time, for example the day the app last detected a fill-up, the day it last did not count a fuel rise and the day of the newest reading kept for the Drives section. If the chart is empty, the reason is given too.

In the event list, each fill-up the app detects from the car's fuel level is noted with the litres added, whether it counts as a full tank (and, if not, why) and what became of it: saved as a new fill-up, added to the detected fill-up before it, or not saved because it repeats one already on file. A rise of more than a litre in the fuel level that the app did not count is noted with its litres and the reason (for example, that no odometer reading came with it), at most once a day for each car.

A section called Launch timing lists the last five times the app started in the foreground and got as far as showing the Dashboard (not those iOS prepared ahead of time or started in the background): the date and time, the app build, the app's language and how many milliseconds the app took to reach each stage of starting up. A line called Widgets says how long ago a Riviera widget last refreshed from the data the app leaves for it, which widget and which size it was, how many prepared states (entries) it got, how many times Riviera widgets refreshed in the hour or less up to then and, if the widget could not show your car or its picture, a short reason in fixed words (for example, that the app has not given it any data yet). The Consumption section, the fill-up lines, the launch timings, the list of background runs and the Widgets line never contain an odometer reading, a price or any other amount of money, a note, a name you typed, a position, your VIN or an account identifier. The launch timings, the list of background runs and the Widgets line stay on this iPhone like the log: they leave it only inside the file you choose to send or inside a backup of your iPhone.

Before an event is saved, Riviera removes anything that looks like an e-mail address, a VIN, a sign-in token, a coordinate pair written with three or more decimals or a number labelled latitude or longitude, a phone number, the random file name Riviera gives an attached document, and any account or person identifier, whether labelled (such as accountId=) or part of a Renault web address. It also removes the exact text of the name you gave the selected car, the number plate you typed for it on the Breakdown card, the names you gave places and the street addresses the app looked up, each if it is at least three characters long and has a letter in it. A new name, plate, place name or looked-up address is picked up from the next event on. The same holds when you switch to another car or sign in for the first time: its name and plate are removed from the next event on. The app never logs your password and is written to avoid logging names, places and street addresses. The removal above covers only the items it lists: it does not recognise other names, place names or street addresses, and because it works by pattern it cannot recognise free text, so read the file before you send it.

You can read the recorded events at any time in Settings → Help & Support → Diagnostics → Diagnostics Log, and the complete file in the review step of Export Redacted Diagnostics. You can clear the log, and with it the crash and hang summaries, the daily figures, the launch timings, the Widgets line, the list of the last background runs, the count of held-back reads and the notes on Renault's last answers to remote commands and to the battery request, with Clear Diagnostics on the Diagnostics Log screen (a new Widgets line is written the next time a widget updates). When you export or report, Riviera writes the file to the app's cache folder (excluded from backups) and removes it when the share sheet or the e-mail draft closes; a copy left behind (for example because the app was closed meanwhile) is removed at the next launch or export once it is an hour old, or at once by Delete All My Data. Clear Diagnostics does not touch that copy. Delete All My Data removes the log, that copy, the launch timings, the Widgets line, the list of the last background runs and those notes along with everything else.

Where the app sends network requests

Riviera communicates only with:

  • Renault's services — the Gigya sign-in and the Kamereon vehicle API, to operate your car's connected features (vehicle data, and the commands you send), and, only when you tap Check vehicle alerts (Settings → Help & Support → For Testers, shown only after seven taps on the version number in Settings → About Riviera) or Check again after it, one request for the list of alerts Renault keeps for your car, at most once every 10 minutes per car and never in the background; Riviera keeps the answer's result, when it was checked, the HTTP status and the names (never the values) of up to 40 fields Renault's answer carried, and, for each alert, only its identifier, type, severity, title, status, closure reason, mileage and next due date and mileage, never any contact details the answer may hold, until you use Delete All My Data; Renault's public websites for the dealer locator and the accessories catalogue (the dealer locator receives a position rounded to about 100 metres and a search radius, the accessories catalogue receives your car's model codes, the country site you reach shows your market, and neither receives your VIN; the accessories catalogue Riviera has read is kept in memory only, for up to 6 hours and at most four catalogues, one per model and market, so reopening the screen asks nothing again; it is never written to the iPhone, and Delete All My Data empties it); and the web addresses Renault's API supplies for your car's pictures, from which Riviera downloads the picture the Dashboard shows and, unless you added your own picture, the front three-quarter picture for the widgets (the Apple TV app downloads the Dashboard's picture too, if you use it). These websites and the hosts of the pictures also see your IP address, as with any web request, and each picture is requested from exactly the web address Renault's API supplies for it. When the iPhone downloads a picture it does not check the address for your VIN or remove anything from it, so if the address contains an identifier such as your VIN, the server that serves the picture receives it. The only check is on the copy of the address saved for Apple TV, which is left out if it contains the VIN. Riviera connects to the Gigya sign-in and the Kamereon vehicle API only when the server's certificate chain is valid and includes one of the certificates or keys built into the app (until June 2027, after which this version falls back to the system's normal checks), and it never follows a redirect from them. If the certificate does not match, for example on a work or public Wi-Fi network that inspects secure connections, nothing is sent: the app says it could not confirm Renault's server identity and suggests updating Riviera or trying another network. Refused connections are noted in the diagnostics log with the server's name and how many times it has been refused since the app started, at the first, second, fourth and eighth refusal and so on, so a connection that keeps being refused does not fill the log;
  • Public data providers — fuel prices from national services in France, Spain, Austria and Italy, from the Competition Council's price monitor in Romania (Monitorul Prețurilor Carburanților) and from Tankerkönig in Germany if you add your own key under Data Sources (the info button on the nearby fuel prices screen); fuel stations, EV chargers and dealers from Open Charge Map and OpenStreetMap (the latter through the public Overpass servers overpass-api.de, overpass.private.coffee and ovp.dresel.systems; the last holds Europe only); weather from Open-Meteo; and lists of recent earthquakes from the EMSC-CSEM SeismicPortal (www.seismicportal.eu) or, only when it gives no usable answer, from GFZ's GEOFON service (geofon.gfz.de). Which fuel service is asked depends on where the search is. Riviera works out on the iPhone, from country outlines built into the app, which country the search point is in; no request is made for this. It asks that country's service first, then your Settings country's service if that country's border is within the search area or no more than about 3 km beyond its edge, then OpenStreetMap, which has station locations but no prices. A service that has nothing there or cannot be reached hands over to the next. When you search from a screen, Riviera does not always wait for the price services to fail or come back empty: if none of them has given any stations 3.5 seconds after the search began, Riviera asks OpenStreetMap as well. It uses that answer only if none of the price services has stations in the end, and it cancels the request as soon as one of them answers with stations. A fuel search that goes to OpenStreetMap also does not try the Overpass servers strictly one after another: if a server has not answered 1.5 seconds after it was asked, Riviera asks the next one as well, and if a server fails it asks the next one at once; the first usable answer is used. One search can therefore reach up to three servers (each at most once, each with the same position, rounded to about 100 metres, and the same radius), and Riviera cancels any request still under way when an answer arrives. Charger and dealer searches try the Overpass servers one after another. Fuel, charger and dealer searches carry the search radius and a position rounded to about 100 metres: your car's parked position, the centre of the map you are viewing or, for the dealer locator and the EV charger search, your phone's position (if you have allowed location). Weather lookups carry only your car's position rounded to about 10 km. Earthquake requests carry no position at all, neither your car's nor your phone's. Apart from its start time, which follows this iPhone's previous check (30 minutes before it, and at most 24 hours back), the request is the same for every user: the earthquakes of magnitude 4.5 or more recorded since that time in one fixed area covering most of Europe and the Mediterranean (27° to 72° north, 32° west to 50° east). They are sent only while Earthquakes nearby is on, notifications are allowed and your car's position is known, never in Demo Mode, when the app refreshes your car's data and during a background refresh (see below). Riviera keeps the answer in memory only, for up to 10 minutes, and does not ask again within that time while the app keeps running; it works out on the iPhone how far each earthquake was from your car, as the table above describes. Spain's and Italy's fuel data are whole-country files that carry no position or radius; Riviera keeps a copy of each country's station list (public data only) in its cache folder and reuses it for 30 minutes (Spain) or 3 hours (Italy) before downloading it again. Answers from the weather service, the Romanian price monitor, OpenStreetMap and the dealer locator are kept in memory for 2 to 30 minutes, and the full-screen map and the EV Charging Stations screen keep, in memory, the fuel stations and chargers they have shown until you close them, so the same search is not sent again. The nearby fuel prices screen also keeps its last list on the iPhone, as the table above describes. These requests never carry your name, e-mail, VIN or plate, and, as with any web request, the provider can see your IP address. Each request to these providers, and to Renault's dealer locator and accessories catalogue, names the app: it carries the app's name and version, the address of this policy and the developer's support e-mail, so the operator can tell what software is asking and reach the developer. That label says nothing about you, your car or your iPhone beyond "iOS". Your own Tankerkönig key, if you add one under Data Sources, is kept in the Keychain and sent to Tankerkönig whenever Tankerkönig is asked in the order above, which happens only for a search in Germany or, when Germany is your Settings country, near its border; without it, German stations come from OpenStreetMap through Overpass. Riviera includes its own Open Charge Map key, the same for every user, so it does not identify you to Open Charge Map; only if a build has no key of its own does it send instead a key you pasted into an earlier version of Riviera, if there is one (Delete All My Data removes it);

Riviera asks Apple's geocoder to turn your car's parked position, and the places in Parking History, into a street address, and uses Apple Maps to draw maps and to search for dealers. Unlike the public providers above, Apple receives exact coordinates for these (the search centre, your car's parked spots and, if you have allowed location, your phone's position once at first sign-in, to find your country). Apple's own privacy policy applies.

If you use iCloud Sync, Shared Garage or Apple TV sharing, it may also update those iCloud copies, for example with a refill it detected and saved automatically. While notifications are allowed, it may also contact Open-Meteo with your car's position rounded to about 10 km (when a weather alert is on; the hail alert is on by default) and, when your fuel is low and the low-fuel alert is on (it is by default), the first fuel service in the order above for the place where the car is parked, with that position rounded to about 100 metres, to name the nearest station. Riviera never tracks your phone's location in the background.

Riviera has no developer-side database. Your data is held on your device (and in any backup of your iPhone), by Renault for your connected-car account, and in the iCloud, Apple TV, Calendar, Reminders and Shared Garage features you choose to use. When a feature needs it, your car's position is sent to Apple as exact coordinates and, together with your IP address, to Renault's dealer locator and the public map, charger and fuel-price services listed above, rounded to about 100 metres, and to Open-Meteo for weather, rounded to about 10 km. For dealer and charger searches your phone's position is handled the same way. The one-time country check sends your phone's position to Apple only. The earthquake services receive no position at all, only your IP address with the request. Those services keep their own records under their own policies.
